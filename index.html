Fee Mechanics — Glossary

TER — Total Expense Ratio
The flat annual fee charged by a traditional ETF, expressed as a percentage of assets. Deducted regardless of performance. Typically very low (under 0.20%).
Management Fee
The annual fee charged by an alternatives fund manager. Applied to opening NAV at the start of each year — the investor does not earn returns on this amount.
Performance Fee
A fee charged only when the fund generates returns above a defined threshold. Designed to align the manager's incentives with investor outcomes.
Hurdle Rate
The minimum return the fund must achieve before a performance fee applies. Protects investors from paying incentive fees on ordinary returns.
Soft Hurdle
Once the hurdle is cleared, the performance fee applies to all gains above the high-water mark — not just the excess above the hurdle. More favourable to the manager.
Hard Hurdle
The performance fee applies only to gains above the hurdle amount. The manager earns nothing on the hurdle portion of returns. More favourable to the investor.
High-Water Mark (HWM)
The highest NAV the fund has previously reached. Performance fees can only be charged on gains above this level — protecting investors from paying twice on the same ground.
Catch-Up Clause
After the investor receives their preferred return (hurdle), the manager receives a disproportionate share of subsequent profits until they hold their target percentage of total profits.
Core / Satellite
A portfolio construction approach where a low-cost index core (e.g. SPY) anchors the portfolio, and alternative funds serve as satellite positions seeking differentiated returns.
